Exam technique

How to answer a 9-marker

What separates Level 3 from Level 2 on 9-mark Assess questions.

Assess means weigh and decide

AQA defines "Assess" as considering the arguments for and against a particular issue and making a judgement. This means you need to build arguments on both sides and then decide which is stronger. A 9-mark question tests four things: Knowledge (AO1), Application (AO2), Analysis (AO3), and Evaluation (AO4). Evaluation is worth 3 of the 9 marks — it is the single biggest assessment objective.

Find the endpoint

Every 9-marker has a starting issue and an endpoint. Before you write, identify both.

If the question asks about profit and your chain stops at "sales increase" — you have not finished. Your chains on both sides must reach the exact endpoint the question asks about.

Your final judgement must also address this endpoint directly. Weighing up general pros and cons is not enough.

A recommended structure

A clear way to organise your answer:

Argument FOR — develop a chain from the issue to the endpoint using the case study. Add a small qualification if you can.

Argument AGAINST — develop a counterargument, again reaching the endpoint.

Weighted judgement — decide which side is stronger, explain why, and answer the exact question.

This structure is not compulsory — a student who embeds evaluation throughout can do just as well. But it makes the necessary thinking visible.

Level 3 vs Level 2

Level 1 (1–3 marks)Some knowledge and an attempt at context, but analysis is missing or very limited. Evaluation is assertion — stating a conclusion without explaining why.
Level 2 (4–6 marks)Relevant knowledge, some good application, basic chains of reasoning. There may be evaluation with some support, but the response does not genuinely weigh the arguments against each other.
Level 3 (7–9 marks)Accurate knowledge, strong application throughout, logical chains reaching the endpoint. Evaluation effectively weighs competing arguments and reaches a convincing judgement that directly answers the question.

The 6–7 boundary

The difference between Level 2 and Level 3 is not about writing two sides. It is about the quality of the analysis and the overall judgement.

The key question: has the student moved from explaining arguments to genuinely weighing competing considerations and using that reasoning to answer the exact question?

A conclusion that just summarises both sides is not weighing. You need to explain which argument is stronger and why.

Common mistakes

Stopping the chain too earlyYour argument ends at "sales increase" when the question asks about profit. You need to take it all the way.
Summarising instead of weighingYour conclusion repeats both sides but does not decide which matters more, or why.
Generic applicationYou mention the business name but the context does not actually drive the argument.
Bolt-on evaluationWriting "However, it depends on many factors" adds nothing. Name the specific factor and explain why it matters.

Quantitative 9-markers

Some 9-mark questions include a required calculation. Six of the nine marks relate to quantitative skills:

AO1 (2 marks) — knowing the formula and selecting the right figures.
AO2 (2 marks) — performing the calculation correctly.
AO3 (1 mark) — interpreting and using the result in your argument.
AO4 (1 mark) — using the result to support your judgement.

The remaining 3 marks (AO3 = 1, AO4 = 2) come from further qualitative analysis and evaluation. A correct calculation alone, with no analysis or evaluation, would be placed at the bottom of Level 2 (4 marks).

Own figure rule: if you make a calculation error but correctly interpret your own result, you can still receive credit for the analysis and evaluation. Do not panic if you think your number is wrong — explain what it means and use it.
