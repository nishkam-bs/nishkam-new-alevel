Exam technique

How to answer a 6-marker

What separates Level 2 from Level 1 on 6-mark Analyse questions.

One argument. That's it.

AQA's senior examiner says: "Only one argument is needed for top marks." Do not waste time writing two or three separate points. Pick the strongest argument you can make and develop it fully.

A 6-mark question tests three things: Knowledge (AO1) — do you understand the concept? Application (AO2) — can you use it in the context of the business given? Analysis (AO3) — can you build a chain of reasoning from the concept to the outcome the question asks about?

Find the endpoint

Every 6-marker has a starting factor and an endpoint. Before you write anything, identify both.

If the question asks about profit and your chain stops at "sales increase" — you have not finished. If it asks about PED and you end with "demand increases" — that is not PED. If it asks about market share, more sales alone might not be enough because the total market could also be growing.

Your chain must land on the exact endpoint the question asks about.

Use the case study properly

Simply writing the business name is not good application. The examiner's test is: could you remove the business name and the answer still works identically? If yes, your application is weak.

Good application means using specific facts, numbers or circumstances from the case study to drive your explanation. For example, SHEIN having nearly 10,000 employees makes coordination particularly important — that is the context doing work in the argument.

Level 2 vs Level 1

Level 1 (1–3 marks)Some relevant knowledge. Perhaps an attempt to apply it. But the chain of reasoning is missing or does not connect the starting factor to the endpoint. Often generic — could be about any business.
Level 2 (4–6 marks)Accurate understanding. The case study context is genuinely used to drive the explanation. A clear chain of reasoning links the starting factor to the exact endpoint the question asks about. No significant missing step.

What you do NOT need

There is no AO4 on a 6-marker. You do not need:

— a counterargument or "however"
— advantages and disadvantages
— a conclusion or judgement
— an introduction
— a definition (though you can include one if you want)
— more than one argument

Common mistakes

Stopping too earlyYour chain ends at "sales increase" when the question asks about profit. You need to explain what happens to profit.
Generic answerYou write about teamwork in general without using anything specific about the business in the case study.
Missing step"Lean production lowers costs" — but how? The examiner cannot fill in the reasoning for you.
