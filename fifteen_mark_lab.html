Exam technique

How to answer a 15-marker

Evaluate two options, then recommend one.

What the question wants

Every 15-mark question asks you to evaluate two options and recommend one. Sometimes you are given the two options (Region A or Region B). Sometimes you choose them yourself ("Evaluate two ways…"). If you choose, pick two that clearly fit this business.

The marks are AO1 = 3, AO2 = 3, AO3 = 4 and AO4 = 5. Evaluation is worth the most, so the quality of your judgement is what moves you up the levels.

The structure we use

1. Argument for the first option. Build a chain with 5–6 analysis connectives: this means that, as a result, therefore, this could lead to, causing. Use the case study and a worked figure. Finish with a sentence that uses the words of the question.

2. Evaluation of the first option. Start with "However…". Think about cost, risk, what the data does not show.

3. Argument for the second option. Same again: connectives, case study, data, a model if it helps.

4. Evaluation of the second option.

5. Conclusion. Overall, I would recommend… → Although…, … carries greater weight because… → However, this ultimately depends on… → Therefore, [answer in the words of the question].

AQA does not insist on this order, so the lab checks it separately and it never changes your mark. It is the most reliable way to hit every AO.

Questions with a calculation

Six of the fifteen marks are for quantitative skills: formula and figures (AO1, 2), the calculation (AO2, 2), using the result in an argument (AO3, 1) and using it in your judgement (AO4, 1).

Do the calculation first and show your working. Then ask what the figure means for this business and which option it supports.

If you do not use the calculation, the answer cannot get above 9. AQA says this in its mark scheme.

Own figure rule: if your arithmetic goes wrong but you use your figure sensibly, you still get credit for the analysis and evaluation.

The levels

Levels 1–2 (1–6)Mostly knowledge, little use of the case study, basic or missing chains. Judgement is asserted or has limited support.
Levels 3–4 (7–12)Developed chains, at Level 4 for both options with the case study used throughout. A judgement with some balance, but not fully weighed or not tied to the business's priorities.
Level 5 (13–15)Well-developed chains for both options, case study used throughout, and a recommendation that weighs both options and fits this business's situation. Models used well are typical here.

What holds answers back

Only one option properly analysedBoth options need a developed argument. Otherwise the maximum is 9.
No recommendationThe question says "recommend". Without one, the maximum is 9.
Summarising instead of weighingSaying the good and bad points of each again is not a judgement. Explain why one carries greater weight. Otherwise the maximum is 12.
A judgement that could be about any businessLevel 5 needs a recommendation that fits this business's situation and priorities.

Models and theory

Ansoff, Porter's Five Forces, the Boston Matrix, PED, Maslow, Herzberg and break-even are typical of Level 5 answers when they move your argument on. Use one where it explains why, not as a label.

A good recommendation is honest about its weaknesses. Say what it depends on and what could go wrong — the same humility we try to show when we make any decision that affects other people.
