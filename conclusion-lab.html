    How conclusions are judged

    On a 9-mark or 15-mark question, the conclusion is where a lot of the evaluation marks are decided. Strong conclusions make a clear judgement that comes out of the arguments already in the answer and explain what that judgement depends on. Weak ones repeat the paragraphs, sit on the fence, or bring in something new at the end.

    There is no set wording you have to use. Your conclusion is judged on what it does, not on whether it starts with a particular phrase.

    What a strong conclusion does

    It answers the exact question. If the question asks whether the business should raise its price, the judgement says whether it should raise its price, not whether the business will be successful.

    It weighs the two sides. Don't summarise both paragraphs and then pick one. Say which side carries more weight for this business and why.

    It says what the answer depends on, and why. "It depends on the business" isn't enough. Say what about this business: how price sensitive its customers are, whether the forecast can be trusted, how big the risk is compared with its profit.

    It ends with a clear final judgement that matches everything before it.

    It doesn't bring in new arguments. If an idea hasn't been developed in your paragraphs, the conclusion is not the place for it.

    9-mark Assess questions

    The question asks about one issue, such as whether to spend more on something or whether to raise a price. AQA wants a decision on that issue. For the top level there has to be a clear answer that weighs the argument against the counter-argument. A judgement can be qualified ("yes, as long as…") but it still has to answer the question.

    If there is a calculation, use the result in your judgement. Say what the figure shows and why it matters more, or less, than the evidence on the other side. You can also question how reliable the figure is.

    15-mark Evaluate questions

    You either evaluate two options you are given, or two ways you have chosen to solve a problem. Either way you must recommend one. "Both are good" or "they should do both" is not a recommendation.

    The top level considers what matters most to this business in its situation, for example profit now or growth later, keeping control, or how much risk it can take. A recommendation that leaves out the main weakness of the option you chose is unlikely to reach the top level.

    If the question has a calculation, use the figures in your recommendation.

    Levels, not marks

    The lab gives your conclusion a level using the AQA level descriptors for that type of question. 9-mark questions have three levels and 15-mark questions have five. It is a judgement of your conclusion only, so it is not the same as a mark for a whole answer.

    When questions open

    Some questions are homework questions. They open here after the homework is due, so you always write the full answer yourself first.
